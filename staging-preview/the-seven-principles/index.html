Simple answer

What are the Seven Noahide Laws?

The Seven Noahide Laws are a set of universal moral principles associated with the biblical story of humanity and the covenant with Noah. In simple language, they speak to what a just, humane, and peaceful society requires: reverence for G-d, the sanctity of life, justice, honesty, compassion, respectful relationships, and the protection of the vulnerable.

The framework

Seven laws, seven foundations

1. No Idolatry

Respect for the One Creator and a life that is not governed by false gods or manufactured idols.

2. No Cursing G-d

Words matter. We are called to reverence God and avoid blasphemy or contempt toward what is sacred.

3. Don’t Murder

Human life is sacred. This principle is the foundation of civilization itself.

4. No Illicit Relationships

Family, intimacy, and boundaries matter because human beings are relational and vulnerable.

5. Don’t Steal

Property, fairness, and honesty are necessary for trust and social stability.

6. No Animal Torture

Compassion toward living creatures is a moral demand, not a luxury.

7. Uphold Justice

Justice is not optional. Every society needs laws, courts, accountability, and a way to resolve wrongs fairly.

Where found?

Where are the Seven Noahide Laws found?

The phrase “Seven Noahide Laws” is a later formulation and a modern way to summarize a larger moral and biblical tradition. The biblical story approaches this subject from the beginning of humanity, especially through Adam and Noah. We try to be careful about distinguishing what the text explicitly says, what Jewish tradition emphasizes, and what later interpreters have organized into a clear list.

A seeker-first lens

You don’t need a label to begin.

Whether you call this a biblical framework, a set of universal principles, or simply a way of asking what makes a life good, the point is the same: these ideas are meant to help people live better with one another and with G-d.
