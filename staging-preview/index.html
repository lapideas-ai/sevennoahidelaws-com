In the Beginning

Did the Almighty create humanity without a plan or directions?

Long before today’s religions, the Bible describes principles given to all humanity — beginning with Adam and continuing through Noah. This is one of the earliest ideas in the biblical story: a moral framework meant for people, not only for a single nation or a single institution.

The Seven Noahide Laws are a way to begin asking serious questions without needing to adopt a label first. The real question is not “am I in the right religion?” It is: “What makes a life good, just, and humane?”

What are the Seven Noahide Laws?

Seven universal principles for a good life.

The simple answer: the Seven Noahide Laws are seven foundational principles for living a just, humane, and peaceful life. They are about reverence for G-d, human life, justice, property, family boundaries, compassion for living creatures, and the moral structure of society.
✦
One Creator There is one G-d, and life has meaning because of it.
✦
Sacred Speech What we say about G-d and to one another matters.
✦
Life Human life is sacred and not ours to take.
✦
Family Boundaries protect love, trust, and dignity.
✦
Honesty & Property What belongs to someone else stays theirs.
✦
Compassion Cruelty toward living creatures has no place in a good life.
✦
Justice Fair courts and moral accountability matter.

Where are the 7 Noahide Laws found?

In the beginning. And in the stories that follow.

The biblical story

The biblical text describes a universal moral framework beginning before the later divisions of religions. Adam, Noah, and the covenant story are central to this understanding.

Traditional formulation

The Seven Noahide Laws are a later traditional formulation, and they should be understood carefully: scripture, tradition, interpretation, and modern teaching are not all the same thing.

You may already believe this

You may already believe this.

  • • Honesty matters, even when no one is watching.
  • • Cruelty to people or animals is wrong.
  • • Justice matters, even when it is inconvenient.
  • • There is something greater than ourselves.
